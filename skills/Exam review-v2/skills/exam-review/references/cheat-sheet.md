# Mode: CHEAT SHEET

A condensed overview of what has been covered so far on a topic or unit.
Orientation, not a re-teach. This is what he reads on the walk to the exam.

## Structure

1. **One-paragraph map** — what this unit is about and how its pieces relate.
2. **Term bank** — every term covered, one line each, in the course's wording.
   **Grouped by section, not alphabetized** — the grouping is what makes the
   structure of the unit visible, and alphabetizing destroys exactly the
   information he's here for.
3. **Formulas** (quantitative courses) — every formula, each variable defined,
   one line on **when it applies**. Applicability is what gets missed under
   time pressure, not the algebra.
4. **Distinctions table** — every commonly-confused pair in the unit with the
   one-line test that separates them.
5. **Weak spots** — the items in this unit he has actually missed, pulled from
   `gaps.md` by **filtering on the chapter field**, not by reading the whole
   log. Marked clearly, so a review pass starts here instead of at the top.
   Ignore anything under `## Archive` — those were resolved at a past exam.

## Rules

- Dense. No analogies, no worked examples, no re-explanation — those live in
  the original `slide-breakdown` responses, and duplicating them here makes a
  document too long to use as a last-pass reference.
- If he asks for it as a file, write markdown and present it.
- If coverage is thin because little has been logged, say what's missing
  rather than padding it out from general knowledge.
